1Introduction & Background

Project Overview

This project builds a 12-month cohort retention matrix from raw transaction tables, tracking what percentage of customers acquired in a given month returned in each of the subsequent 11 months. The output feeds directly into the marketing team's CAC payback period and LTV projection models.

2Initial Setup · Cohort Assignment Query

Cohort Assignment — First Purchase Month

cohort_assignment.sql
-- Assign each customer to their acquisition cohort month WITH first_purchase AS ( SELECT customer_id, DATE_TRUNC('month', MIN(order_date)) AS cohort_month FROM orders GROUP BY customer_id ), customer_activity AS ( SELECT o.customer_id, fp.cohort_month, DATE_TRUNC('month', o.order_date) AS activity_month, EXTRACT(MONTH FROM AGE( DATE_TRUNC('month', o.order_date), fp.cohort_month )) AS month_number FROM orders o JOIN first_purchase fp ON o.customer_id = fp.customer_id ) SELECT cohort_month, month_number, COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) AS retained FROM customer_activity GROUP BY 1, 2 ORDER BY 1, 2;
3Insights & Analysis

Retention Findings

The cohort matrix revealed significant month-1 retention variation across acquisition channels, and identified a "leaky bucket" pattern where 60% of all churn occurred within the first 60 days of acquisition.

  • Month-0 to Month-1 retention averaged 34% across all cohorts — with email-acquired customers retaining at 48% vs. 21% for paid social.
  • The August 2023 cohort showed anomalously high retention (52% at Month 3) coinciding with a loyalty programme pilot launch.
  • By Month 6, average retention stabilized at ~12%, suggesting a core loyal segment emerges and persists beyond the initial churn window.
  • CAC payback modelling indicated the business recovered acquisition cost in Month 4.2 on average for email-channel cohorts.
